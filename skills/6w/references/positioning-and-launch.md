# Positioning and the first launch

Read this when rendering the marketing section for a commercial or community project, when the user asks how to describe or announce the product, or when Who's distribution rating is below 3. Adapted from the `marketing-strategy-pmm` and `launch-strategy` skills in alirezarezvani/claude-skills (MIT, credited in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`). The frameworks are theirs. The rule that every line below is a hypothesis until a customer says it back is 6W's.

## Positioning in six steps

Work from the records that already exist. Ask only for what is missing.

1. List the alternatives the beachhead persona uses today: direct competitors, adjacent tools, and doing nothing. The Why broken-workaround rationale has most of this.
2. Name what only this product does, from the What core loop and anti-scope. If nothing is unique, say so; the position then rests on fit, price or trust, not on a feature.
3. Map each unique attribute to what the persona gets from it, in the persona's words from `evidence-from-interviews.md`.
4. Name the customers who care most. That is the beachhead persona, or it should be.
5. Choose the frame: head-to-head with a named alternative, a niche inside an existing category, or a new category. New categories are expensive to explain; prefer the first two for a first release.
6. Name the trend or event that makes now the moment, from the Who switching trigger and the When "why now".

Write the result as one statement and store it in the primary persona's card and in `MARKETING.md`:

```text
For <beachhead persona> who <need in their words>, <product> is a <frame>
that <key benefit>. Unlike <the alternative they use today>, it <primary difference>.
```

The statement is validated when several target customers describe the benefit unprompted in their own words. Until then it carries `evidence: assumption`, however good it sounds.

## Message angles, objections and proof

For the primary persona, write two or three message angles, each tied to one pain or one trigger. For each objection in the persona card, write the response and the proof the product can show. A proof is a `REQ-` (the product does it), an `EXP-` (a test will show it) or a `SRC-` (someone said it). An objection with no proof is a `RISK-`.

Hypothetical copy is never a customer quote. Label it as the builder's draft.

## Channels: owned, rented, borrowed

Map every channel in the persona card to one of three kinds, and make sure the plan has all three:

| Kind | Examples | What it costs | When to start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owned | A waitlist, a mailing list, the product itself | Building the list | Now, before anything else |
| Rented | Communities, social platforms, forums the persona reads | Playing by their rules; a post the community rejects is a lost channel | Once there is something to show |
| Borrowed | Newsletters, podcasts, partners, launch sites | Relationships built weeks ahead | Two to six weeks before the launch |

A plan that uses only rented channels (post it on Reddit) is incomplete. Record the owned channel as a `REQ-` or `TASK-` if the product does not have one.

## The launch as a sequence, not a day

| Phase | What happens | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before (two to six weeks) | Waitlist or early access open, borrowed-channel outreach, assets made | `TASK-` records with dates in `DELIVERY.md` |
| Launch day | A time-boxed checklist, every channel used, the builder present to answer | An `EXP-` with the sign-up or activation threshold and the decision rule |
| After (thirty days) | Follow-up content, comparison against the alternative, a note to the list, retargeting where there is budget | `TASK-` records; the retention `EXP-` |

Gate the date on readiness: if the owned channel or the assets are not ready a week out, move the date rather than launch into silence. Record the slip as a decision.

## Internal tools and community projects

Positioning still applies: the stakeholder or the contributor has alternatives too. Replace price with the cost of their time, replace channels with the meetings, chats and repositories where they already are, and replace the launch with the first team that adopts it. Skip anything about paid acquisition.

## What not to do

- Do not write a market size, a competitor price or a conversion rate without a dated `SRC-`.
- Do not claim superiority ("the fastest", "the only") unless a `SRC-` or `EXP-` supports it.
- Do not plan a launch for a product whose When says it cannot ship by the date.
