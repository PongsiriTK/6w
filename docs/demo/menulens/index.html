WHY 94/100 OK

Allergy travelers cannot tell what is safe to order from a menu they cannot read

pain-severity 4/4 × 30 user

For a traveler with celiac disease or a nut allergy, every restaurant meal abroad is a risk decision. The user eats out 2 to 3 times a day on a trip and reports skipping meals or eating only packaged food on past trips. One bad dish costs a day of the trip.

Sources: SRC-001 SRC-002

broken-workaround 4/4 × 25 source

Google Lens translates words literally: it renders a dish name but not what is in it. Printed allergy cards get a nod from staff who do not read them carefully. Asking the waiter fails across the language barrier. Three linked r/Celiac threads describe the same pattern.

Sources: SRC-002

value-capture 3/4 × 25 assumption

Hypothesis: a one-trip pass at 4.99 USD or 19.99 USD a year. The allergy traveler already spends on cards and apps. Cost per trip estimated at roughly 0.14 USD in model calls (see ASM-002). Willingness to pay is untested.

Links: ASM-001 ASM-002 EXP-001

success-metric 4/4 × 20 user

Success: at least 60 percent of scans end with the user tapping "I ordered this" within the session. Kill signal: under 20 percent after the first 200 users, or more than two reports of a flagged dish that was unsafe.

WHAT 95/100 OK

Photo of a menu to dish cards with allergen flags for the user's profile, plus a local-language question card

core-loop 4/4 × 30 user

MenuLens turns a photo of a menu into dish cards that say what each dish is, its main ingredients, and which of the user's allergens it may contain, then gives a phrase in the local language to confirm with staff. One photo, one answer, one question to ask.

Links: REQ-001 REQ-002

Four features with acceptance criteria: allergy profile setup (REQ-001), menu photo to dish cards with flags (REQ-002), local-language confirmation card (REQ-003), cache of the last 20 scanned menus for offline re-reading (REQ-004).

Links: REQ-001 REQ-002 REQ-003 REQ-004

workflow-fit 4/4 × 25 assumption

The traveler already has the phone out to photograph the menu. A link on the home screen that opens the camera matches that moment with no store download on hotel Wi-Fi.

Links: PERSONA-001

device-and-environment-constraints 4/4 × 25 user

Restaurants abroad mean roaming data and basements with one bar of signal. Images are compressed on the device before upload, results are cached, and the app degrades to the cached menus when offline (EDGE-002). Large text and labeled controls for one-handed use at a table.

Links: EDGE-002 REQ-004

WHETHER 94/100 OK

unit-economics 4/4 × 30 assumption

At 12 scans a trip that is about 0.14 USD against a 4.99 USD pass. Monthly model cost at 100, 1,000 and 10,000 active users with one trip each: about 14, 140 and 1,400 USD, before hosting.

Links: ASM-002 RISK-003

technical-bottlenecks 3/4 × 25 assumption

Scan latency of 6 to 10 seconds at a table is tolerable once, painful three times. Handwritten and chalkboard menus fail often (EDGE-001). Provider rate limits at launch are untested.

Links: EDGE-001 EXP-002

legal-privacy-security 4/4 × 25 user

RISK-001: allergen flags are informational, never a clearance. Every card carries "confirm with staff" and the local-language question. Photos are processed and not stored. Issues to investigate before launch: consumer-protection wording for health information in the user's jurisdiction (Australia) and the EU for European users, and the model provider's terms on food images. Owner: the builder, before the first fifty users.
